# Assignment 5 – Unconsidered Metrics (Recommendation of an Additional Vital Sign)

## Overview

This assignment recommends an additional vital sign not currently present in the emergency triage dataset that would enhance the triage process. The chosen vital sign is **Peripheral Oxygen Saturation (SpO₂)**.

## Recommended Vital Sign

**Peripheral Oxygen Saturation (SpO₂)**

## Definition

SpO₂ is a vital sign that measures the percentage of haemoglobin in the blood carrying oxygen. It provides a quick, non‑invasive snapshot of how effectively the heart and lungs are delivering oxygen to the body.

## Clinical Ranges

| SpO₂ Range | Category | Clinical Implication |
|------------|----------|----------------------|
| 95 – 100% | Normal | Adequate oxygen level |
| 90 – 94% | Mild hypoxia | Reduced oxygen |
| 85 – 89% | Moderate hypoxia | Significant hypoxaemia |
| < 85% | Severe hypoxia | Requires immediate intervention |

## Importance in Triage

SpO₂ is significant because it serves as an early warning marker for respiratory or circulatory compromise. By identifying abnormal oxygen levels before more obvious symptoms appear, clinicians can prioritise care, escalate treatment, and prevent life‑threatening deterioration. This makes SpO₂ not only a routine measurement but also a critical tool for rapid decision‑making in emergency and clinical settings.

## Limitations and Sources of Error

SpO₂ readings can sometimes be misleading due to factors that interfere with accuracy:
- Cold hands, poor circulation, or patient movement
- Nail polish, artificial nails, or strong ambient light
- Conditions such as carbon monoxide poisoning (causes falsely elevated SpO₂ because the device cannot distinguish between oxygen and other gases bound to haemoglobin)
